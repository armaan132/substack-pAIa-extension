Revisiting the approaches with Google Scholar queries for each:

1. **Framing language analysis**
   - **Google Scholar Query**: [citation framing language sentiment analysis scholarly communication](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=citation+framing+language+sentiment+analysis+scholarly+communication)
   - **State of the art libraries**: Partially available. Named entity recognition and keyword extraction can identify descriptive terms around citations. Some sentiment lexicons include reputation/authority markers.
   - **Original research needed**: Creating specialized lexicons of citation framing terms and their sentiment polarities specific to academic or journalistic contexts.

2. **Citation context analysis**
   - **Google Scholar Query**: [citation context analysis author sentiment toward sources](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=citation+context+analysis+author+sentiment+toward+sources)
   - **State of the art libraries**: Basic tools exist for extracting citation contexts (sentences around citations). General NLP libraries can identify syntactic relationships.
   - **Original research needed**: Developing models specifically trained to distinguish author commentary from cited content and to classify the rhetorical function of citation contexts.

3. **Citation pattern analysis**
   - **Google Scholar Query**: [citation network analysis source reliability trust patterns](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=citation+network+analysis+source+reliability+trust+patterns)
   - **State of the art libraries**: Network analysis tools can track citation frequencies and relationships.
   - **Original research needed**: Creating metrics that meaningfully correlate citation patterns with source trustworthiness assessments. Developing models to distinguish between different purposes of repeated citation.

4. **Specialized NLP approaches**
   - **Google Scholar Query**: [citation function classification stance detection source evaluation](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=citation+function+classification+stance+detection+source+evaluation)
   - **State of the art libraries**: General stance detection and discourse analysis frameworks exist but aren't citation-specific.
   - **Original research needed**: Training citation-specific sentiment classifiers with annotated datasets of author attitudes toward sources. Adapting discourse analysis to the specific rhetorical moves authors make when evaluating sources.

Each query is designed to focus on the intersection of citation analysis and author attitudes toward sources, which should help you find relevant research on these specific approaches.
